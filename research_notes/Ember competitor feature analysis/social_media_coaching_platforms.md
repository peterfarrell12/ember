## How do they turn coaches into distribution?

### Takeaway
This is the best-evidenced part. All four pay for referrals, but on very different terms: HubFit 30% recurring for 12 months, Everfit 10% for 24 months, Kahunas a flat $49. On top of that, Everfit uses a permanent free tier, Kahunas and HubFit use white-label apps that put the product in front of every client, and HubFit and Everfit target creators with challenge and subscription features.

### Cited Findings

HubFit
- Affiliate programme: 30% of every payment a referred coach makes, recurring for 12 months from first payment; monthly and annual plans and upgrades count, add-ons do not. Attribution: sign-up within 90 days of a link click, or a discount code at checkout; neither expires. Payout: monthly bank transfer, $100 minimum, 30-day hold for refunds. Seen 24 Nov 2026 — [HubFit affiliate](https://hubfit.com/affiliate)
- Referred coaches get 15% off their first 3 months — [HubFit affiliate](https://hubfit.com/affiliate)
- Target affiliates are named: fitness marketing agencies, business coaches and consultants, coach mentors and certification providers, fitness creators, YouTubers, podcasters and newsletters. Applicants need not be customers and are reviewed. Existing HubFit coaches use a separate "Refer & Earn" programme — [HubFit affiliate](https://hubfit.com/affiliate)
- Illustrative earnings on the page (not guarantees): about $20.70 a month per Premium coach, $35.70 per Ultimate coach, 50 Ultimate coaches gives $1,785 a month or $21,420 over 12 months — [HubFit affiliate](https://hubfit.com/affiliate)
- Rules: no self-referral, no bidding on HubFit's name in search or social ads (paid traffic to the affiliate's own content is allowed), no coupon sites — [HubFit affiliate](https://hubfit.com/affiliate)
- Creator positioning: a "content creators" solution page pitches challenges and leaderboards to drive Instagram-led acquisition; a white-label app page exists — [HubFit content creators](https://hubfit.com/solutions/content-creators); [HubFit white-label](https://hubfit.com/white-label-fitness-app)
- Trial, not free tier: 14-day free trial, no card; plans from $39 a month (Standard, up to 50 clients); clients always free — [HubFit blog](https://hubfit.com/blog/top-coaching-platforms-for-online-personal-trainers)

Kahunas
- Referral programme: "$49 commission" per referred coach once they sign up through the link and stay active for 60 days, paid to PayPal. Source is the help-centre article as quoted in search results; the article itself returned 404 after redirecting to help.kahunas.app on 24 Nov 2026, so current terms are unconfirmed — [Kahunas help: referral program](https://help.kahunas.io/en/articles/236-kahunas-referral-program)
- White-label is the core product: coaches get a branded client app; Kahunas publishes coaches' apps under its own bundle namespace (example `com.cofox.kahunas.PeakPerformanceCoaching`) — [Stripe case study](https://stripe.com/customers/kahunas); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.cofox.kahunas.PeakPerformanceCoaching&hl=en)
- Pricing is per seat, from $5.00 down to $0.50 as seats grow, 14-day free trial; a competitor notes the branded app unlocks at 200 seats — [Kahunas home](https://kahunas.io/); [HubFit blog](https://hubfit.com/blog/top-coaching-platforms-for-online-personal-trainers)
- Trial conversion rose from 50% to 65% and churn fell from 16% to 7% in under a year (2023) — [Stripe case study](https://stripe.com/customers/kahunas)

Everfit
- Affiliate programme: 10% of revenue from each verified paid user for the first two years; 30-day cookie; covers Pro and Studio plans and add-ons; no cap; PayPal monthly, $25 minimum; net of processing, conversion and payout fees. Seen 24 Nov 2026 — [Everfit affiliates](https://everfit.io/affiliates/)
- Affiliates are told to share links on "social media, YouTube, TikTok, or blog platforms" and are given media resources and comparison pages — [Everfit affiliates](https://everfit.io/affiliates/)
- Free tier as acquisition: Starter plan is free with no time limit and no card, up to 5 clients; paid plans have a 30-day free trial without a card — [Everfit llms.txt](https://everfit.io/llms.txt)
- Education as acquisition: Everfit Academy with free courses and free CECs, plus webinars — [Everfit llms.txt](https://everfit.io/llms.txt)
- Creator partnerships appear as case studies (Rachel Henley, 500,000+ followers); her clients earn "shareable progress badges" that encourage referrals — [Everfit case study](https://everfit.io/?p=2465)

peak/performance
- Runs both an affiliate programme ("earn recurring commission on every subscription you help create", rate not published, "real-time payouts", UTM tracking) and a separate influencer programme whose application form asks for follower bracket (under 10K up to 1M+) and promises to "put the platform in your hands first" and "build content together". Seen 24 Nov 2026 — [peak/performance affiliates](https://peakperformance.to/affiliates); [peak/performance influencers](https://peakperformance.to/influencers)

### Inferences
- HubFit's affiliate targeting (business mentors, certification providers, agencies) is the sharpest idea here: one business coach who mentors 50 online coaches is worth more than 50 individual referrals. Its split of "affiliate for non-customers" and "Refer & Earn for customers" keeps the two audiences from colliding.
- Commission generosity runs opposite to brand size: HubFit (newest of the three, unfunded per Tracxn) pays 30%, venture-backed Everfit pays 10%. A challenger buys distribution with margin.
- Everfit's free 5-client tier and Kahunas's low per-seat entry price both remove the cost objection for a coach with no clients yet; HubFit instead relies on a short trial plus a discount through affiliates.
- White-label apps spread the coach's brand, not the vendor's, so they do not obviously market the platform to end clients; the distribution effect is coach-to-coach ("what app is that?").

### Gaps
- No named ambassador, big-name coach partnership or co-branded campaign was found for HubFit or Kahunas.
- No Facebook group, Skool community, live event or podcast was found for any of the four.
- Terms of HubFit's customer "Refer & Earn" and peak/performance's commission rate: not published on the pages read.
- How many affiliates each has, and what share of sign-ups they drive: not public.

## What coaches say about choosing or leaving each platform

### Takeaway
In the reviews found, coaches move on price and speed, not on social content. Price rises are the main reason to leave; a fast, modern app is the main reason to arrive.

### Cited Findings
- HubFit reviewers on Capterra cite speed and ease ("the main reason I moved over from Trainerize"), Trainerize being "dated and slow", and other platforms being too expensive — [Capterra HubFit](https://www.capterra.com/p/10004923/Hubfit/)
- Everfit reviewers cite user-friendliness and looks; complaints include weaker white-label branding than Trainerize — [Capterra Everfit reviews](https://capterra.com/p/202837/Everfit/reviews/)
- A Kahunas leaver wrote that Kahunas "double in price"; others criticised the workout builder — [Capterra Kahunas reviews](https://www.capterra.com/p/10028886/Kahunas/reviews/)
- A third-party comparison reports Kahunas's App Store rating at 3.3 from 73 ratings and Everfit's at 4.5 from 172; HubFit self-reports 4.9 (2026) — [Coachway: Kahunas alternatives](https://coachway.io/articles/kahunas-alternatives/)

### Inferences
- Because switching is price-driven, a competitor's price rise is a marketing moment: comparison pages and affiliate discount codes are positioned to catch coaches at exactly that point.

### Gaps
- No Reddit threads (r/personaltraining and similar) or independent coach YouTube reviews were surfaced; Capterra reviews are anecdotal and can be vendor-solicited.

## What a small Irish or UK team can copy first, and what is hard

### Takeaway
Copy the cheap, structural moves first: a recurring affiliate programme aimed at coach-mentors, a free start with no card, self-ranking comparison pages kept current, and time-saved case studies. Large free education academies, creator case studies at 500K followers and founder networks are the expensive parts.

### Cited Findings
- Cheap to copy: recurring affiliate commission with a discount code for the referred coach (HubFit: 30% for 12 months, 15% off 3 months) — [HubFit affiliate](https://hubfit.com/affiliate)
- Cheap to copy: separate programmes for customers ("Refer & Earn") and for non-customer partners such as mentors, certification providers and agencies — [HubFit affiliate](https://hubfit.com/affiliate)
- Cheap to copy: comparison articles with a named author and a recent "updated" date (HubFit, updated 16 Nov 2026) — [HubFit blog](https://hubfit.com/blog/top-coaching-platforms-for-online-personal-trainers)
- Cheap to copy: case studies framed on admin time saved, with a named coach (HubFit) — [HubFit customers](https://hubfit.com/customers)
- Cheap to copy: an influencer application form that collects handles and follower bracket (peak/performance) — [peak/performance influencers](https://peakperformance.to/influencers)
- Costs money each month: a permanent free tier for up to 5 clients (Everfit), since free coaches still consume hosting and support — [Everfit llms.txt](https://everfit.io/llms.txt)
- Expensive: an academy with free accredited CEC courses and regular webinars (Everfit) — [Everfit llms.txt](https://everfit.io/llms.txt)
- Hard to replicate: creator partners with 500,000+ followers (Everfit's Rachel Henley story) and a founder's pre-existing coach network (Kahunas) — [Everfit case study](https://everfit.io/?p=2465); [Stripe case study](https://stripe.com/customers/kahunas)
- Local opening: Kahunas lists no language other than English, and TrueCoach payments are limited to the US, UK, Australia and Canada (so not Ireland), per competitor comparison pages — [Coachway: Kahunas alternatives](https://coachway.io/articles/kahunas-alternatives/); [HubFit blog](https://hubfit.com/blog/top-coaching-platforms-for-online-personal-trainers)

### Inferences
- Brand follower counts of 19K to 28K for companies claiming 15,000 to 50,000 coaches suggest a small team should not judge itself on brand-account followers. Distribution through other people's audiences (affiliates, mentors, coaches' own apps) is what the evidence supports.
- For Ember, the items that need the owner's decision before copying are the commission rate, any free tier or free AI limit, and anything with a monthly cost; these are pricing decisions.
- An Ireland-and-UK angle (euro and sterling payments, local food data, local coach mentors as affiliates) is a gap none of the four address on the pages read.

### Gaps
- No data on which of these tactics actually drives sign-ups for any of the four; the ranking above is judgement from what they invest in publicly.
- Cost of running each programme (affiliate payouts, academy production) is not public.
