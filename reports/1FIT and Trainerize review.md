# 1FIT and Trainerize: what they are, what they charge, and where Ember stands

Read on 25 November 2026 from each company's own site and App Store listing. Their numbers are their own claims and were not checked independently. Nothing here comes from using either product.

## The short version

Both are tools a coach pays for and hands to paying clients. Neither is an app a person can download and use alone: 1FIT says its client app works only for clients a coach has onboarded ([App Store, 1FIT Client](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/1fit-client/id6450225170)), and Trainerize is sold the same way ([trainerize.com](https://www.trainerize.com/)). That is the opening for Ember: a free app the whole audience can use, where these two only ever reach the fraction who pay for coaching.

On the coach's side they are well ahead of Ember. Ember's coach view exists in the prototype and is switched off; both of these have client payments, check-in forms, messaging, branded apps and wearable sync running today.

## 1FIT

A UK online coaching platform, founded February 2023 by Aaron Biddle and Ben Martin ([1fit.com/about](https://1fit.com/about/)). It claims "5,000+ personal trainers and coaches" and "60K+" clients on one page and "thousands" on another, so treat the figures loosely.

**What a coach gets** ([platform](https://1fit.com/platform/), [nutrition](https://1fit.com/platform/nutrition-coaching-software/), [check-ins](https://1fit.com/platform/check-in-management/), [payments](https://1fit.com/platform/payments/), [wearables](https://1fit.com/platform/wearable-integrations/)):

- Workout programme builder with 200+ exercise videos, supersets, planned training blocks, last week's lifts shown to the client, and weekly volume per muscle.
- Nutrition: macro targets by training and rest day, meal plans, a food database of "over 3 million" or "over 5 million" foods (its pages disagree, and neither names the source), barcode scanning, 300+ recipes, supplement plans, and a MyFitnessPal link.
- Check-ins with a form builder, reminders, and feedback by video or text.
- Habits as daily checklists, phased roadmaps with milestones, progress photos with comparisons.
- One-to-one messaging on every plan; group chats from the second plan up.
- Client payments through Stripe: subscriptions, staged payments, free trials, failed-payment retries.
- Wearables through Apple Health, Health Connect and Garmin, which also brings in Oura, WHOOP, Polar and Fitbit.
- Custom app theming, team seats, automations and Zapier on the higher plans.

**What it does not show:** any AI feature, photo or voice food logging, challenges, leaderboards or community groups. None is mentioned on the pages read.

**Price** ([pricing](https://1fit.com/pricing/)): £40 a month for up to 10 clients, then £4 a client; £60 with payments and group chats; £175 for up to 75 clients; £295 for unlimited. Seven-day free trial. Clients pay nothing to use the app.

**The client app:** rated 3.9 from 87 ratings on the UK App Store, 98 MB, English only. Reviews ask for better use of wearable data and for calories to come from Apple Health or MyFitnessPal.

## Trainerize

The biggest name in the category. It claims "400k+ personal trainers and coaches" and "1.6M+ clients" ([trainerize.com](https://www.trainerize.com/)). It serves solo trainers through to gym chains.

**What a coach gets** ([features](https://www.trainerize.com/features/), [nutrition](https://www.trainerize.com/nutrition-coaching/), [branded apps](https://www.trainerize.com/features/custom-branded-fitness-apps/)):

- An AI workout builder, custom programmes, an on-demand workout library, and video coaching with live calls.
- Nutrition: a meal planner that writes three to seven day plans, grocery lists, 2,400+ recipes, a 400,000-food database (source not named), barcode scanning, meal photo tracking, and a MyFitnessPal link.
- Habit coaching with streaks and badges.
- Groups, challenges with leaderboards, a workout of the day, badges and milestones.
- Messaging one to one and in groups, voice messages, automatic messages.
- Payments through Stripe, session packs, and products to sell.
- A custom branded app, on the higher plan listed in the stores under the coach's own name.
- Apple Watch app, Apple Health, Garmin, Fitbit and Withings.

**Price** ([pricing](https://www.trainerize.com/pricing/)), in US dollars: free for one client; $9 a month for two; from $23 a month for five clients, rising with client count to 200; from $248 a month for studios. Several things cost extra: nutrition coaching $20 to $45 a month, payments $10, video $10, and $169 to set up a branded app. Thirty-day free trial. The pricing page contradicts itself in places (it also quotes $25 and $275 for the same plans), so check before quoting it.

**The client app:** rated 4.9 from about 16,000 ratings on the UK App Store, but 512 MB. Recent negative reviews call the meal tracker "a disaster" and report crashes.

**Affiliates** ([affiliate programme](https://www.trainerize.com/affiliate-program/)): 15% recurring commission, open to influencers, educators and business coaches.

## What they have that Ember lacks

All of it sits on the coach's side or on the phone:

1. Client payments and subscriptions.
2. Check-in forms a coach designs, with feedback by video.
3. Exercise demonstration videos.
4. A coach's own branding on the app.
5. Live sync from Apple Health, Health Connect and Garmin; Trainerize also has an Apple Watch app.
6. Group chat and, at Trainerize, video calls.
7. Custom habits a coach sets for a client.
8. Automations: scheduled messages, onboarding steps, reminders.

## What Ember has that neither offers

1. An app with no coach required and no charge.
2. Voice and photo logging free for the person using it.
3. Named, labelled food data for Ireland and the UK.
4. A deeper game: quests, bosses and leagues, not only streaks and badges.
5. Squads that work for a following of thousands without each person being a paying client.

## What this means

This part is judgement, not sourced fact.

- **For a creator with a large following,** these platforms serve the few hundred who buy coaching. Ember can serve everyone else for free, and a squad gives the creator a weekly board for that whole audience. The two are not in competition today.
- **When coach mode opens,** payments, check-in forms and programme delivery are the minimum a coach will expect, because both of these already have them. Ember's advantage would be that the client already uses the app daily for free.
- **Price is where coaches hurt.** Both charge by client count, and Trainerize charges separately for nutrition, payments and video. A flat or free coach tier would stand out, but pricing is the owner's decision.
- **The cheapest gaps to close without coach accounts** are custom habits and exercise videos. Videos need footage, which costs money or a partner who records them.
