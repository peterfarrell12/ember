# Ember already gives away rivals' paywalls

Ember's free app already includes most of what these six competitors, and the wider market, charge for. Barcode scanning, photo and voice logging, an AI assistant and measured energy expenditure are paid features at MyFitnessPal, Lose It!, Cronometer and MacroFactor, and ParrotPal, the closest UK rival, offers nothing lasting for free at all: a 7-day trial, then roughly £39.99 a year. What Ember lacks is mostly phone plumbing (reminders, widgets, live Apple Health and Health Connect sync, an Apple Watch app) plus a short list of cheap features the two admired calorie apps have: a fasting timer, forgiving streaks, a weekly review, PDF and CSV export, meal plans and recipe import. Two of the six dictated names could not be pinned to one product: "Oxcal.app" is probably 0xCal (spelt with a zero) and "Peach Peak Coach OS" is either peak/performance or CoachOS, so please confirm both before anything is built against them. On social media, the honest finding is that the evidence does not show these six running large or unusually skilled accounts: no follower or view counts could be verified for the calorie apps, and the coaching platforms' brand accounts sit at roughly 19,000 to 28,000 Instagram followers. Their growth comes from other things: a founder talking to camera (ParrotPal), fast public shipping (0xCal), and affiliate commissions, free starter tiers and self-written comparison pages (HubFit, Kahunas, Everfit). The viral calorie-app videos that define this category follow the playbook of Cal AI, whose own founders say copying it is the easiest way to waste money. The plan at the end has five steps, starting with what makes Ember feel like a proper phone app, and it leaves pricing, free AI limits, wearable order, consent wording, tracking and any monthly spend as your decisions, each with a recommendation.

All research was done on 24 November 2026. Prices are as published on that date; vendor feature lists are the vendors' own claims and were not tested hands-on.

The six split into two different kinds of product. ParrotPal and 0xCal are calorie apps a person downloads for themselves, which is what Ember's free app competes with today. HubFit, Kahunas, Everfit and the Coach OS candidates are tools sold to coaches; their member apps cannot be used without a coach, so they compete with Ember's coach mode, which is still switched off.

### ParrotPal sells a coaching programme wrapped around one chat box

ParrotPal's home screen is a single box that takes typing, voice, a photo or a barcode, with a nutrition-label scan as the fallback when a barcode is not found ([parrotpal.com](https://parrotpal.com)). Around that sits a programme: **"Coach PAL" sends one message a day** about what you ate, in a tone you choose (Gentle or Tough Love); a **weekly check-in resets next week's calorie and protein targets**; a "LeanShield" score out of 100 sums up protein, training and eating enough; and four "real life" modes (Restart, Pause, Holiday, Recharge) stop time away from wrecking your averages ([parrotpal.com](https://parrotpal.com)). It also has a GLP-1 medicine and dose journal, workout plans, home and lock screen widgets, Apple Health sync, and PDF and CSV export "for doctors, PTs, or coaches" ([parrotpal.com](https://parrotpal.com)), plus 16,000+ recipes, recipe import, streaks, weekly reports and podcasts ([App Store US](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6462694790)). A new user gets a 7-day challenge with daily tasks straight after signing up ([screensdesign](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/ai-calorie-counter-parrotpal)).

It is rated **4.7 from about 4,100 UK ratings** ([App Store UK](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/id6462694790)), and it is a funded UK company with a Cambridge professor as lead scientific adviser ([parrotpal.com](https://parrotpal.com)). Its weak point is billing: reviewers report being charged 39.99 after cancelling a trial the same day, and a paid plan not being recognised ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/parrotpal.com)). Its claim of "850,000+ users" is the company's own and sits oddly beside roughly 8,000 ratings across the UK and US ([parrotpal.com/about](https://parrotpal.com/about)). It is English only and not on Android ([parrotpal.com](https://parrotpal.com)).

### 0xCal wins on speed and native iPhone craft

0xCal is a lean logger: you chat with an assistant called "Gram", take a photo, speak, or scan a barcode, all through one sheet ([0xcal.app](https://www.0xcal.app)). What sets it apart is how deeply it uses the iPhone. On phones with a depth sensor, **"Ruler+" measures the plate while you take the photo**; label reading happens on the phone itself; a fasting timer (12:12, 14:10, 16:8 or custom) can block apps during a fast using Screen Time; and water and caffeine each have a daily budget ([0xcal.app](https://www.0xcal.app); [App Store US](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6749210009)). It has an Apple Watch app, widgets, Siri and Shortcuts actions, two-way Apple Health sync, a streak "that survives late logging", weekly meal planning, and import of your history from Lose It!, Lifesum, Fitatu or a spreadsheet ([App Store US](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6749210009)). It shows its working (portions in grams, each meal split into ingredients) and needs no account ([0xcal.app](https://www.0xcal.app)). It ships in 13 to 14 languages ([App Store US](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6749210009)).

It is also tiny: **4.1 from 47 US ratings** and 5.0 from 2 in Ireland ([App Store US](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6749210009); [App Store Ireland](https://apps.apple.com/ie/app/id6749210009)). It is a design and engineering reference for Ember, not a market threat. Fibre tracking and custom foods were still user requests in January 2026 ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/0xcal)), both of which Ember already has.

### The coaching platforms compete on price, native apps and where the AI sits

**HubFit** is the best-liked of the three confirmed platforms: 4.8 from 393 App Store ratings and 5.0 from 390 Trustpilot reviews ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/hubfit/id6448201245); [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/hubfit.io)). Coaches get programmes with 5,000+ exercise videos, 5,000+ recipes, check-ins, habits and messaging with voice and video notes ([HubFit Coaching](https://hubfit.com/platform/coaching)), plus a challenge engine with leaderboards, badges and branded share cards, and a private community feed ([HubFit Engagement](https://hubfit.com/platform/engagement)). Clients get native iPhone and Android apps with a barcode food tracker, automatic personal-record tracking and sync with Apple Health, Health Connect, Garmin, Fitbit, Oura and WHOOP ([HubFit Integrations](https://hubfit.com/platform/integrations)). HubFit hands AI to the coach's own Claude or ChatGPT through a connector released on 22 October 2026 ([HubFit changelog](https://hubfit.com/changelog)). Taking payments from clients needs the $69 plan ([HubFit pricing](https://hubfit.com/pricing)).

**Kahunas** puts AI inside the product. Every plan gets AI-written daily summaries per client and an "attention score" that flags who needs a nudge ([Kahunas insights](https://kahunas.io/features/insights)); larger plans add AI assistants, lab-report tracking, menstrual-cycle tracking, pose-tracking exercise analysis and sellable courses ([Kahunas pricing](https://kahunas.io/pricing)). Its client app is now a web app installed from the coach's link, with offline logging ([Kahunas web app](https://kahunas.io/features/web-app)), and Apple Health reaches it only through a separate companion app ([Kahunas wearables](https://kahunas.io/features/wearables)). Kahunas relaunched on 28 October 2026 with per-seat pricing ([HubFit comparison page](https://hubfit.com/compare/kahunas-alternative)), and **November 2026 brought a wave of one-star reviews** about login failures, lost data and forms that would not submit ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/kahunas.io)).

**Everfit** is the broad San Francisco incumbent, launched in August 2020 ([Everfit help centre](https://help.everfit.io/en/articles/5622629-more-integrations)), with a free tier for up to 5 clients that never expires. Its 2026 releases closed its nutrition gap: "MacroSnap" AI meal scanning from a photo, barcode or label in July, micronutrients in August, AI meal plans and a bookings calendar in September, WHOOP in October ([Everfit product updates](https://blog.everfit.io/product-update)). Clients get an Apple Watch app, challenges with live leaderboards and a community forum ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/everfit-train-smart/id1438926364); [Everfit automation](https://everfit.io/automation/)). Its catch is add-ons: automation from $29, payments from $9 and full meal plans from $39 a month sit on top of the plan price ([Everfit pricing](https://everfit.io/pricing/)), so a 50-client coach wanting all three pays about $172 a month by the published table. Coaches complain about exactly that, and about app stability; its Trustpilot score is 3.0 from 18 reviews against 4.7 from 2,700 App Store ratings ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/everfit.io); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/everfit-train-smart/id1438926364)).

**peak/performance** claims the widest bundle of all (built-in video calls, a marketing studio, a sales pipeline, AI meal-photo logging, a hormone-testing module), but none of it could be verified in a live product, and its own pages contradict each other on wearables: the home page says "Native", the FAQ says they "are not available yet" ([peak/performance FAQ](https://peakperformance.to/faq)). **CoachOS** is a lean tool built around an AI helper that flags which clients are slipping ([CoachOS features](https://os-coach.com/features)). Neither has reviews anywhere.

Three blind spots are shared. HubFit and Kahunas offer the member no AI at all: no photo, voice or chat logging ([HubFit Coaching](https://hubfit.com/platform/coaching); [Kahunas nutrition](https://kahunas.io/features/nutrition)). None of the platforms names its food database, so coverage of Irish and UK products is unknown. And HubFit and Kahunas are both registered in the UAE and price in dollars ([HubFit terms](https://hubfit.com/terms-and-conditions); [Kahunas terms](https://kahunas.io/trust/terms-coaches)); neither markets EU data storage.

### The wider market charges for the shortcuts Ember gives away

The big calorie apps charge for whatever saves time on each log. MyFitnessPal put its barcode scanner behind the paywall on 1 October 2022 ([Digital Trends](https://digitaltrends.com/phones/myfitnesspal-barcode-scanning-not-free-premium-subscription)) and still lists barcode scanning, meal scanning and voice logging as paid features at £39.99 to £64.99 a year in the UK ([App Store GB](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/myfitnesspal-calorie-counter/id341232718); [MyFitnessPal](https://www.myfitnesspal.com/premium-plus)). Cronometer keeps the scanner free but charges for photo logging, the fasting timer, recipe import and PDF reports ([Cronometer](https://cronometer.com/gold/)). MacroFactor, the app known for a calorie target that adapts to your measured expenditure, has no free tier at all ([App Store GB](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/macrofactor-macro-tracker/id1553503471)). Meal planning is paid everywhere it appears. **No app in the research gives away an AI coach or chat, voice logging, meal planning or an adaptive calorie target.** Ember already gives away the first two and measures energy expenditure, which is the working part of the fourth.

The lesson for "standard for free" is about permanence. Four years on, MyFitnessPal's scanner decision is still the lead line in rivals' marketing ([Foodnoms](https://foodnoms.com/vs/myfitnesspal)), and Strava's move of leaderboards behind its subscription drew the same reaction ([Cycling Weekly](https://cyclingweekly.com/news/latest-news/bitterly-disappointing-or-fair-price-for-a-great-service-how-cycling-weekly-readers-react-to-major-strava-changes-456227)). Taking a free feature away costs more goodwill than never offering it. Set Ember's free line once, at a level the AI budget can carry for good.

## Ember lacks phone plumbing, not headline features

Set against all six, Ember's free app is already ahead on the things people pay for elsewhere, and it has game mechanics (quests, XP, a league, boss challenges) that none of the six goes beyond streaks, badges and coach-run challenges to match. The gaps fall into five groups. The "work" and "matters" ratings are this report's judgement, not sourced facts; "running cost" means a cost that recurs with use, chiefly AI calls against your daily spending ceiling.

### Group 1: matters most, moderate work, no AI cost

These are what make an app feel like it belongs on a phone, and both admired calorie apps have all of them. Reminders, native scanning, native speech and health sync are already listed for the App Store phase of Ember's build plan; widgets are the addition.

| Feature Ember lacks | Who has it | Work | Running cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reminders and push notifications | ParrotPal, 0xCal, HubFit, Kahunas, Everfit | Moderate | None per use |
| Live Apple Health and Health Connect sync | ParrotPal (Apple), 0xCal (two-way), HubFit, Everfit | Moderate | None per use |
| Home and lock screen widgets | ParrotPal, 0xCal | Moderate, native code | None |
| Native barcode scanner and speech in the phone app | All the native apps | Moderate | None |

Health sync carries rules, not costs. Apple forbids using health data for advertising and forbids storing personal health information in iCloud ([App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)), and under UK data protection law health data needs explicit, separate consent ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/special-category-data/what-are-the-rules-on-special-category-data/)). Ember's no-trackers, EU-only stance already fits both.

### Group 2: matters, small work, no running cost

These are the cheapest wins and the safest things to make free for good, because each costs nothing per use.

| Feature Ember lacks | Who has it | Free elsewhere? | Work |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intermittent fasting timer | 0xCal | Paid at MyFitnessPal, Cronometer, Lose It! | Small |
| Forgiving streaks and Pause or Holiday modes | ParrotPal, 0xCal | Not covered | Small |
| Weekly review that resets targets, with a weekly report | ParrotPal | Adaptive targets are paid (MacroFactor) | Small: Ember already measures weight trend and expenditure |
| PDF and CSV export | ParrotPal | Paid at MyFitnessPal and Cronometer | Small |
| Sharing cards (a streak, a record, a challenge result) | HubFit, Everfit | Not covered | Small |
| Vitamins and minerals | HubFit, Everfit, Kahunas | Free at Cronometer | Medium: Ember's UK food file would need rebuilding to carry them |
| Import history from other calorie apps | 0xCal | Not covered | Small to medium |
| In-app voting on what to build next | 0xCal | Not covered | Small |

Sources: fasting, streak and importers ([App Store US, 0xCal](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6749210009)); pause modes, weekly check-in and export ([parrotpal.com](https://parrotpal.com)); share cards ([HubFit Engagement](https://hubfit.com/platform/engagement); [Everfit case study](https://everfit.io/?p=2465)); micronutrients ([HubFit Coaching](https://hubfit.com/platform/coaching); [Everfit product updates](https://blog.everfit.io/product-update); [Kahunas nutrition](https://kahunas.io/features/nutrition)); paid status elsewhere ([Cronometer](https://cronometer.com/gold/); [MyFitnessPal](https://www.myfitnesspal.com/premium-plus)).

Two cautions apply. Sharing cards must respect the rule that progress photos are private by default, so a card should carry numbers, never a photo unless the user adds one. And vitamin and mineral figures will be patchy for branded products, so a missing figure must show as missing, not as zero.

### Group 3: matters, but every use costs money

These are the features where "free" has a bill attached. Each needs a decision on free AI limits before it is promised.

| Feature Ember lacks | Who has it | Free elsewhere? | Running cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Meal plans and weekly meal planning | ParrotPal, 0xCal, HubFit, Kahunas, Everfit | Paid everywhere found | One or more AI calls per plan |
| Recipe import from a link | ParrotPal | Cronometer charges for it | An AI call per import |
| A daily coach message, with a choice of tone | ParrotPal | No app gives an AI coach free | One AI call per active user per day, and needs push first |
| Several photos for one meal | 0xCal | AI photo logging is paid almost everywhere | More image cost per log |

The unit costs are small but real. At Anthropic's list prices on 24 November 2026, a downsized food photo costs roughly $0.0004 on the cheapest current model and about $0.008 to $0.015 on the mid and upper models; a chat message runs from about $0.0005 to $0.018 ([Claude pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing); [Claude vision docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision)). These figures are the researchers' arithmetic from published prices, not measured costs. They mean **a free user scanning three meals a day costs about $8 a year on a mid-tier model and about $0.50 on the cheapest**, so the choice of model, and whether the cheapest is accurate enough on food, decides whether generous free AI is affordable. The market mostly avoids the question by not offering free AI at all; the only metered free allowance found is SnapCalorie's reported three logs a day, and even that figure is disputed ([Macaron](https://macaron.im/blog/best-free-ai-calorie-trackers)).

Restaurant and menu scanning is on your list of things Ember lacks, but **none of the six was found to have it**; 0xCal estimates restaurant meals from a description ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/0xcal)). It can wait.

### Group 4: bigger native builds for later

| Feature Ember lacks | Who has it | Why later |
|---|---|---|
| Apple Watch app | 0xCal, Everfit | A separate native app to build and maintain |
| Depth-sensor portion measuring | 0xCal only | Works only on certain iPhones; native code |
| Siri and Shortcuts actions | 0xCal | Native code; useful once the basics are in |
| Multiple languages | 0xCal (13 to 14), HubFit (10), Everfit (6) | First users are Irish and UK; ParrotPal and Kahunas are English only |
| GLP-1 or medication journal | ParrotPal only | Simple to build, but health-adjacent |

The medication journal deserves care. It fits Ember's rule that health content is general guidance, never diagnosis, as a private log. It should stay out of marketing: in 2026 the UK advertising regulator banned social posts, including paid influencer posts, that promoted prescription weight-loss injections ([Hello Partner](https://hellopartner.com/2026/02/12/asa-bans-public-social-posts-promoting-weight-loss-jabs-in-landmark-ruling)).

### Group 5: people and coach features that wait for their flags

Challenges between people with leaderboards are standard on HubFit, Everfit and (from 100 seats) Kahunas, and a free social feed is normal in workout apps such as Hevy ([JEFIT comparison](https://www.jefit.com/blog/fitbod-vs-strong-vs-jefit-vs-hevy)). Friends and challenges are worth making free, but they bring moderation and privacy work, so they belong after the single-user app has shipped. The rest are coach-mode features for when that flag turns on: client payments through Stripe (all platforms), an AI summary of each client with an attention flag (Kahunas, CoachOS, Everfit), automations and onboarding flows, community feeds, and at the far end lab reports, cycle tracking, form-check video and courses. Ember's coach mode starts with one thing none of them offers together: a member app with AI logging, game mechanics and named Irish and UK food data, with the data kept in the EU.

## Their social media is smaller than it looks

The direct answer to "why is their social media so good" is that, for these six, the evidence does not show it is. What the evidence shows is good distribution, most of which happens away from the brand accounts. This section separates what is evidenced from what is not.

### What is evidenced

**ParrotPal's marketing is one founder with a phone.** Scott Flear posts from his personal TikTok account, talking to camera or screen-recording the app. His hooks are plain: "Stop scrolling if you're tracking your calories" ([TikTok, 28 Nov 2023](https://www.tiktok.com/@scott.flear/video/7306573220239461665)); "Can a photo track calories and get the information right? Lets find out" ([TikTok, 28 Oct 2024](https://www.tiktok.com/@scott.flear/video/7430830531249540384)); a study showing only consistent trackers lost weight ([TikTok, 13 Nov 2023](https://www.tiktok.com/@scott.flear/video/7301012644780068129)). He tags UK rivals (#myfitnesspaluk, #nutracheckapp), replies to comments on video, and tells viewers to search the App Store for the exact app name. ParrotPal's website lists no social accounts and no creator or affiliate programme; it publishes UK guides (Greggs calories, supermarket protein) and "MyFitnessPal alternative" pages, and leans on advisers and awards for trust ([parrotpal.com](https://parrotpal.com)).

**0xCal has no TikTok or Instagram presence that could be found.** Its growth is a Product Hunt launch (fifth of the day on 14 January 2026), a developer who answers reviews within days, in-app feature voting, a release pace that had reached version 1.21 by November 2026, and a self-published accuracy benchmark with "versus" pages ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/0xcal); [App Store US](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6749210009); [0xcal.app](https://www.0xcal.app)). That benchmark is its own testing and no independent test exists.

**The coaching platforms' brand accounts are small.** HubFit shows about 19,000 Instagram followers from 48 posts, Kahunas about 19,000 from 1,299 posts, Everfit about 28,000 ([Instagram @hubfit.io](https://www.instagram.com/hubfit.io/); [Instagram @kahunasio](https://www.instagram.com/kahunasio/); [Instagram @everfit](https://www.instagram.com/everfit/)). These come from undated search snippets. Kahunas posted 27 times as often as HubFit for the same audience, so posting volume is not what built either.

**What does build them is other people's audiences.** HubFit pays affiliates **30% of a referred coach's payments for 12 months** and aims the programme at coach mentors, certification providers and agencies, each of whom can bring dozens of coaches ([HubFit affiliate](https://hubfit.com/affiliate)). Everfit pays 10% for two years ([Everfit affiliates](https://everfit.io/affiliates/)); Kahunas has paid a flat $49 per referred coach, though its current terms could not be confirmed because the help page now returns an error ([Kahunas help](https://help.kahunas.io/en/articles/236-kahunas-referral-program)). Each vendor also writes "best coaching software" articles ranking itself first and refreshes them with the current date; HubFit's was updated on 16 November 2026 ([HubFit blog](https://hubfit.com/blog/top-coaching-platforms-for-online-personal-trainers)). Case studies sell time saved ("cut admin time by 75%") with a named coach ([HubFit customers](https://hubfit.com/customers)), and the one Kahunas TikTok caption retrieved leads with the same pain: "Are you spending more time on admin than actually coaching?" ([TikTok @kahunas.io](https://www.tiktok.com/@kahunas.io/video/7509478976004295954)). Free starter tiers remove the cost objection for a coach with no clients yet.

**The category's viral videos follow Cal AI's playbook.** Cal AI, now owned by MyFitnessPal ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/02/myfitnesspal-has-acquired-cal-ai-the-viral-calorie-app-built-by-teens)), is where the slick calorie-app content comes from, and its founders have described the method in detail. The format is a three-beat "magic moment" (scan, wait, number appears) placed inside videos creators already make, such as "what I eat in a day" ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/jake-castillo-cal-ai-sub-club-podcast-2026)). The biggest hits ran on creators' accounts, not the brand's; a 2025 teardown found "not a single viral tiktok is from their account" ([The Playkit Report](https://playkit.substack.com/p/calorie-tracker-app)). Cal AI signed 300+ creators in 18 months, paid about $1,000 a video, expected about 10 installs per 1,000 views early on, and kept only those who paid back ([FunnelFox](https://blog.funnelfox.com/cal-ai-influencer-marketing/)). Its first test was $2,000 to one weight-loss creator, which brought about 1,000 downloads and 100 paying users in two days ([NetInfluencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/cal-ai-founder-zach-yadegari-breaks-down-the-influencer-playbook-behind-his-apps-growth/)).

That playbook comes with warnings from the people who ran it. Creators with the wrong audience produced "No download spike. Nothing.", and the co-founder's own summary is: "The easiest way for an app founder to blow a ton of money is to copy what I did" ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/jake-castillo-cal-ai-sub-club-podcast-2026)). The economics also depended on a paywall before any free use, which Ember will not have. And Apple briefly removed Cal AI from the App Store in April 2026 over deceptive billing design ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/21/apples-cal-ai-crackdown-signals-its-still-policing-the-app-store/)).

### What is not evidenced

No follower counts, posting frequency or view counts were verified for ParrotPal or 0xCal, because TikTok and Instagram pages could not be read by the research tools. No top posts were measured for any of the six. Nobody checked the Meta Ad Library or TikTok's ad tools, so whether any of them runs paid ads is unknown; that is a gap, not a finding that they do not. No Reddit threads or independent YouTube reviews were found. Nothing shows which tactic drives sign-ups for any of them.

If particular posts impressed you, send two or three links. Reading the profiles by hand on a phone, and searching each company's name in the Meta Ad Library with the country set to Ireland or the UK, would close most of these gaps in an hour.

### The rules that apply in Ireland and the UK

Cal AI's best-performing format barely mentioned the app. That does not transfer here. Irish guidance from the consumer and advertising regulators requires every paid or gifted post to carry a clear #Ad or #Gifted label, visible at once and not buried among hashtags ([CCPC and ASAI guidance](https://www.ccpc.ie/business/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2023/10/CCPC-ASAI-Guidance-on-Influencer-Advertising.pdf)), and the UK regulator's review of more than 50,000 influencer posts found only 57% properly labelled ([Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/en/insights/2026/04/14/asa-and-cap-annual-report-2025-smarter-proactive-advertising-regulation-102mpjx)). UK advertising rules also forbid claims that people will lose a precise amount of weight in a stated time ([ASA](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/weight-control-general.html)). Together with Ember's own rule that nothing fake is presented as real, this means labelled posts, no promised results, calorie figures shown as estimates, and no invented testimonials. ParrotPal's five five-star Trustpilot reviews dated the same day in October 2026 ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/parrotpal.com)) show how a solicited batch looks to an outsider.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. Ember does not need more headline features to beat these six; it needs to feel native on a phone and to be trusted. The two apps you admire most are admired for opposite reasons, one for a humane programme that bends around real life and one for speed and craft, and both are iPhone only, both charge, and both carry tracking on their privacy labels. An app that is free, on Android as well, built on named Irish and UK food data, stored in the EU, free of trackers and honest about which figures are estimates holds a position none of the six occupies. That position is only worth having if the free line never moves, which is why the AI limit is the first decision to make and the hardest to change later.

On the coach side, the lesson from Kahunas's relaunch is that coaches switch on price and reliability, quickly and publicly. When coach mode opens, a member app people already use for free is a stronger opening offer than any feature list, because every platform here asks the coach to bring clients to an app the clients have never seen. The open items are small and specific: two names to confirm, an hour of hand-checking social profiles and ad libraries, and a hands-on look at where ParrotPal's and 0xCal's paywalls sit.
